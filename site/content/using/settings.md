# Settings

The core's OSD options. Every option the core defines is listed here; the list is checked
against the core's own definition on every build.

| Option | Values | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video Output | Auto, Interlaced, Progressive, Native (HDMI) | Auto | The signal the core generates, for HDMI and the analog output alike. **Interlaced** is 480i at 15 kHz, what a standard-definition CRT expects, with proper composite sync (equalizing and serration pulses) so a TV interlaces the two fields correctly; on HDMI the scaler then deinterlaces it (see HDMI Deinterlace). **Progressive** is 480p: every video is resized to the core's 720-pixel-wide picture (a video larger than it is shrunk by averaging the pixels each output pixel covers, so thin lines and text stay even), and on HDMI MiSTer's scaler then resizes that to your display -- two resizes; it is the output for a 31 kHz analog display (a VGA monitor, an external scaler). **Native (HDMI)** is for HDMI only: the core's picture has the video's own pixels -- an 852x480 stream is an 852-pixel-wide picture, not one resized to 720 -- and HDMI's scaler makes the one resize to your display, the sharpest HDMI picture. A picture wider or narrower than your display gets its black bars inside the core's picture where they fit, so a player's subtitles and menus can use them. Every picture the core plays is shown this way, 1280x720 included. Do not use it on an analog display: the analog output carries the same unusual timing (unless `vga_scaler=1`) -- if Auto picks it while a VGA monitor is connected, set `forced_scandoubler=1` in `MiSTer.ini` or choose Progressive. Each new picture size makes MiSTer show its resolution box if `video_info` is set, and with `vsync_adjust=1` or `2` it makes the HDMI mode change, which can blank some TVs for a moment. **Auto** chooses Interlaced when `MiSTer.ini` sets up an analog TV (`composite_sync`, `ypbpr` or `vga_sog` with `vga_scaler=0`, or `direct_video`), Progressive when it sets up a 31 kHz VGA monitor on the core's own signal (`forced_scandoubler` with `vga_scaler=0`), and otherwise Native (HDMI). Auto is decided when no video is playing, so a setting change never switches the signal under a stream. |
| Refresh Rate | Auto, 59.94 Hz, 60 Hz, 50 Hz | Auto | The rate of the signal the core generates. **59.94 Hz** is the NTSC rate (480 lines; 480i or 480p by Video Output). **60 Hz** is the same picture at exactly 60.000 Hz: a game stream (Moonlight) runs at exactly 60 frames a second, and on a 59.94 Hz signal one of its pictures has to be dropped or repeated about every 17 seconds. **50 Hz** is the PAL rate, 576 lines (576i or 576p): for 25 and 50 frames-a-second video, which otherwise judders on a 60 Hz signal; your TV or monitor must accept 50 Hz. **Auto** follows the video: a stream that states a rate of exactly 60, 30, 24 or 120 frames a second gets 60 Hz, one at 50, 25 or 100 gets 50 Hz, anything else (or a stream that states no rate) 59.94 Hz. Auto decides when a stream starts, before its first picture is shown, and keeps that rate until the next stream -- the display may resync once at the start. On HDMI the core's signal goes through the MiSTer scaler: to see 50 Hz video without judder there, the HDMI output must follow the core's rate (`vsync_adjust` in `MiSTer.ini`). |
| Aspect Ratio | Auto, 4:3, 16:9 | Auto | The shape presented to the HDMI display's scaler. **Auto** fits the test pattern to your display (full width on a widescreen HDMI mode); while video plays on the Progressive output with Analog Aspect on Auto the picture fills the core's screen and Auto gives the scaler the video's exact shape (16:9, 4:3, a phone's portrait 9:16 ...), so it is shown undistorted at full sharpness; a stream that declares no pixel shape is taken as square pixels. Whenever the core fits the video inside its 4:3 screen itself (the Interlaced output, or Analog Aspect set to Letterbox or Crop) Auto is 4:3; with Analog Aspect set to Fit, Auto is 16:9 (Fit's screen is meant to be shown at 16:9) -- on a 720x480 or 720x576 HDMI mode, which TVs show at 16:9 by themselves, Auto fills the HDMI picture instead. **4:3** and **16:9** force the shape. |
| Deinterlace | Weave, Bob, Blend | Weave | How a picture that was recorded as two interlaced fields is shown on the progressive output. **Weave** shows both fields together at full resolution; moving edges can comb. **Bob** shows each field on its own, filling the missing lines from the ones above and below: smooth motion, half the vertical detail. **Blend** mixes the two fields: no combing and no flicker, with a soft ghost on fast motion. Progressive video (a game stream, film) is never altered, whatever the setting. A change takes effect immediately. |
| HDMI Deinterlace | Weave, Bob | Weave | Shown instead of Deinterlace while the output is Interlaced: how the HDMI scaler turns the 480i fields back into full frames. **Weave** joins the two fields (full detail; moving edges can comb); **Bob** shows each field on its own, line-doubled. The analog output is not affected: a CRT shows the fields as they are. |
| Anti-Twitter | Auto, Off, On | Auto | Shown while the output is Interlaced. On an interlaced screen each field draws every other line, so a detail one line tall -- a thin horizontal edge, a line of small text, a game's HUD -- is drawn by one field only and flickers at 30 Hz ("twitter"). **On** softens the picture vertically just enough that every such detail is shared by both fields: the flicker stops, at the cost of some vertical sharpness. **Auto** turns it on only for streams that the playing program declares as rendered graphics (a game stream, a desktop, a menu) and leaves ordinary video unfiltered; **Off** and **On** decide for every stream. Video recorded as interlaced fields is never filtered (its fields are already made for an interlaced screen). A program's own screens drawn over the video (its menus, subtitles, a progress bar) are always graphics: Auto and On filter them, Off leaves them sharp. A change takes effect at the next field. |
| Analog Aspect | Auto, Fit, Letterbox, Crop | Auto | How a picture that is not 4:3 is fitted inside the core's 4:3 screen -- what an analog display (a TV, a VGA monitor, an external scaler) shows. **Letterbox** shows the whole picture at its true shape, with black bars above and below (16:9) or at the sides (a 4:3-and-narrower or portrait phone picture). **Crop** fills the screen at the true shape and cuts the picture's edges. **Fit** is for a TV set to its 16:9 (wide) mode, which stretches the core's 4:3 screen to 16:9: the core squeezes the picture to match, so the TV shows it at its true shape -- a 16:9 picture fills the screen, a wider film (2.39:1) has black bars above and below, and a 4:3 picture has black bars at the sides. **Auto** is Letterbox on the Interlaced output; on the Progressive output Auto instead fills the screen and tells the HDMI scaler the picture's shape, so HDMI shows it undistorted at full sharpness -- except a picture wider than the HDMI display (a 2.39:1 film on a 16:9 TV), which the core letterboxes itself so the screen's black bars are part of the core's picture, where a player can show subtitles -- if you watch the Progressive output on an analog display (31 kHz), choose Letterbox (or Crop) instead. A picture within a few per cent of the screen's own size (a DVD-style 720x480 or 704x480) is shown 1:1, unscaled. A change takes effect at the next field. |
| Analog Dither | Off, On | Off | For the analog output on an I/O board whose video DAC has 6 bits a colour (the classic DE10-Nano analog I/O board, "18-bit"): such a DAC shows smooth gradients -- a sky, a fade, a dark scene -- in visible bands. **On** adds a fine, moving dither pattern that the TV averages away, so the gradients come out smooth. Leave it **Off** on a board with a full 8-bit DAC (a SuperStation, a 24-bit analog board): there is no banding to remove. The HDMI output is never affected. A change takes effect immediately. |
| Send Home to the program | | | Passes a "Home" press to the program playing through the core, then closes the menu. The Moonlight client sends it to the PC as the gamepad's Guide (Xbox) button -- the button that opens Steam's overlay or the Xbox Game Bar, which your pad's own Home button cannot reach because it opens this menu. The Jellyfin client and the video player stop what is playing (with nothing playing, they quit); the camera viewer quits. A program that has no use for it ignores it. |
| Program menu | | | Asks the program playing through the core to show its own menu, then closes this one. While something plays, the Jellyfin client and the video player show its title and progress (in the player's list of files, it goes up a folder); the camera viewer shows the list of the camera's modes. The Moonlight client ignores it. |
| Reset | | | Resets the core. |

Every picture size the core decodes is fitted to the screen on the Interlaced and Progressive
outputs: scaled by its true shape (the stream's pixel shape and cropping), centred, with black
bars where the shapes differ -- 640x480, 854x480, a phone's 480x854 or a transcode's 720x404
alike. On **Native (HDMI)** the core's picture is the video's own size instead, and MiSTer's
HDMI scaler fits it to your display ([Video output](../video/output.md)).

## Saved settings

Settings are saved to `config/H264_v1.CFG` on the SD card. If a future version changes
the option layout the file name's version number is bumped and every setting returns to
its default once.
